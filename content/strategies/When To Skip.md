## The arithmetic of skipping

Every question inside a section is worth the same. There is no bonus for the hard ones.

A section gives you roughly 90 seconds per question. Spend four minutes on one hard
question and you have not lost one question — you have lost the two easy ones at the end
of the section that you never reached. That is a net loss of one point at best, and it
happens to strong test-takers constantly, because they are the ones stubborn enough to
believe they can crack it.

## The rule

**At 90 seconds with no clear path forward, mark it and move on.**

Not "when I have nearly got it". At 90 seconds without a path. The feeling of being close
is not reliable, and it is exactly the feeling that eats four minutes.

## The GRE makes this cheap

Within a section you can move freely between questions and there is a review screen. So
skipping costs you almost nothing:

- You can come back with fresh eyes, which genuinely works — you will sometimes see the
  trick in ten seconds that you could not find in three minutes.
- **There is no penalty for a wrong answer.** So before moving on, always put something
  down. An unanswered question is worth exactly zero; a guess is worth more than that.
- Eliminate what you can before guessing. Going from five options to three raises a guess
  from 20% to 33%, and across a test that is real points.

## What to skip on sight

- Geometry with a figure you cannot make sense of within about 20 seconds.
- Reading Comprehension attached to the longest passage, when time is already short —
  do the shorter passages first, since each question is worth the same.
- Combinatorics questions where you cannot tell whether order matters. That confusion
  rarely resolves under time pressure.
- Any question where you have already restarted twice. Two restarts means your reading of
  the problem is wrong, and a third pass usually repeats the same misreading.

## The habit to build

While drilling, notice the moment you think *"I should be able to get this."* That is the
moment the clock starts running away. Recognising that feeling is the skill. The point of
tagging misses as "ran out of time" is to make that pattern visible to you.

## The redefinition you have to accept

In ordinary usage, inferring means drawing a sensible conclusion from incomplete
information. On the GRE it means something much narrower:

**An inference is a statement that must be true given the passage, whether or not the
passage says it outright.**

Not likely. Not reasonable. Not what any thoughtful person would conclude. **Must.**

Almost every missed inference question comes from applying the ordinary meaning instead of
this one.

## The test to apply

For each option, ask: **could this be false while every sentence in the passage remains
true?**

If yes, it is wrong — however sensible it sounds. If no, it is your answer.

This converts a judgement call into a mechanical check, which is exactly what you want
under time pressure.

## What gets people

- **The reasonable extension.** The passage says urban rents rose faster than wages. The
  option says workers were forced to move further out. Plausible, and not forced — they
  might have taken smaller flats, or second jobs, or spent savings.
- **The causal upgrade.** The passage reports a correlation; the option asserts a cause.
  This is the single most common wrong answer in the whole section.
- **The quantity shift.** "Many" becomes "most". "Some researchers" becomes "researchers".
  Read quantifiers as carefully as you would read them in a proof, because that is what
  they are doing here.
- **The extreme.** "Proves", "never", "the sole reason", "all". Academic passages hedge,
  so answers that do not hedge are usually distortions.

## Where the answer usually lives

Correct inference answers tend to be **weaker than you expect** — cautiously worded,
almost disappointingly modest. That is not the test being coy. A weaker claim is harder to
falsify, so it is more likely to be the one that must be true.

When two options remain and one is bolder, the quiet one is usually right.

## The related failure

Answering an inference question with a **true detail** from the passage. It is accurate, it
is right there in the text, and it is not what was asked. That is the same error as solving
for `x` when the question wanted `2x` — see
[Reading The Question Twice](./Reading%20The%20Question%20Twice.md).

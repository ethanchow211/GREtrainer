## What the test actually gives you

The current GRE, minus the essay:

| Section | Questions | Time | Average per question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verbal 1 | 12 | 18 min | 90 s |
| Verbal 2 | 15 | 23 min | 92 s |
| Quant 1 | 12 | 21 min | 105 s |
| Quant 2 | 15 | 26 min | 104 s |

The averages hide the real structure: some questions should take 30 seconds and some
should take three minutes. The averages are a budget, not a target.

## Where the time should actually go

**Verbal.** Reading Comprehension questions attached to a long passage need the passage
read first, so the first question in a set is expensive and the rest are cheap. Budget
roughly 3 minutes to read a long passage, then 60 seconds per question on it. Text
Completion and Sentence Equivalence should average well under 60 seconds — that is where
you buy the time that Reading Comprehension spends.

**Quant.** Aim for 60 seconds on anything routine. That banks time for the three or four
genuinely hard questions in each section.

## Two checkpoints

Do not watch the clock continuously; it is corrosive to concentration. Check it twice.

- **At the halfway question**, you should have slightly more than half the time left.
- **With 5 minutes left**, stop solving and make sure every remaining question has an
  answer marked. There is no penalty for guessing, so a blank is a strictly wasted point.

## Section-adaptive scoring, and what it means for you

The second section of each measure is harder or easier depending on how you did on the
first. Two consequences:

- **The first section matters more than it feels like it does**, because it determines
  which scoring band you can reach at all.
- **If the second section feels brutally hard, that is good news.** It means you did well
  on the first. Test-takers routinely rattle themselves at exactly the moment they should
  be encouraged.

## The pacing failure that is not about pacing

Most "ran out of time" is not slow reading. It is a handful of questions that ate three
minutes each. Fixing pacing is usually about
[When To Skip](./When%20To%20Skip.md), not about going faster on everything.

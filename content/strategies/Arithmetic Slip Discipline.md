## The thing nobody wants to hear

If a meaningful share of your quant misses are tagged "arithmetic slip", more practice on
the topic will not help you. You already know the topic. You are losing points to
handwriting, working memory, and hurry.

This is good news. Concept gaps take weeks to close. Slips close in days, because the fix
is mechanical.

## Where slips actually come from

Almost all of them come from four places:

- **Mental arithmetic you did not need to do.** Holding a partial product in your head
  while reading the next line is where digits get dropped.
- **Cramped writing.** Work squeezed into a corner produces a 4 that reads as a 9 and a
  minus sign that vanishes.
- **Distributing a negative.** `-(x - 3)` becomes `-x - 3` more often than any other
  single error on this test.
- **Not writing the intermediate result down**, then re-deriving it slightly differently
  the second time.

## The fixes, in order of value

1. **Write every step on its own line, left-aligned.** Not for tidiness. So that when your
   answer is not among the options you can find the bad line in five seconds instead of
   starting over.
2. **Never distribute a negative in your head.** Write the parentheses, then write the
   expansion. Two seconds against the most common error on the test.
3. **Circle what the question actually asked for** before you start. See
   [Reading The Question Twice](./Reading%20The%20Question%20Twice.md).
4. **Estimate before you compute.** If you expect roughly 40 and get 400, you catch a
   place-value slip instantly. Three seconds, and it catches a whole class of errors that
   otherwise look perfectly plausible.
5. **Keep numbers small as long as possible.** Cancel before multiplying. `(48 x 35) / 21`
   is unpleasant; cancelling to `48 x 5 / 3`, then `16 x 5`, is not.

## Why the trap options make this expensive

Wrong options on the GRE are not random. They are the results of specific errors. Drop a
sign and your answer is sitting right there in the list, looking like confirmation. You
will not notice the slip, because the test is built so that you do not.

This is exactly why estimation is worth so much. It is the only check that is independent
of the mistake you might have just made.

## A drill worth doing

For one session, drill quant under a rule: write every step, and estimate before
computing. You will feel slower. Compare your accuracy against a normal session. The time
lost writing is almost always less than the time you currently lose re-doing questions you
got wrong.

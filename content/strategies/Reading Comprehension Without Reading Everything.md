## The mistake

Reading a GRE passage the way you would read a textbook — trying to absorb and retain the
content. The passages are deliberately dense, often about a field you do not know, and
there is no time for that.

You are not tested on the subject. You are tested on **what the passage is doing**.

## Read for structure

On the first pass, look for exactly three things:

1. **The main claim.** What is the author arguing? Usually one sentence, and usually not
   the first one.
2. **The turn.** Where does the passage change direction? Watch for "but", "however",
   "yet", "nevertheless". GRE passages nearly always contain a pivot, and the questions
   cluster around it.
3. **The author's stance.** Are they endorsing, criticising, or presenting a debate
   neutrally? Most academic passages are more measured than test-takers assume.

Details — dates, names, numbers — should be *located*, not memorised. You can always go
back for them, and going back is faster than trying to hold them.

## The reading itself

- **Skim the first sentence of each paragraph** to get the shape, then read properly.
- **Note in your head what each paragraph does**, in about four words: "sets up old view",
  "objection", "author's alternative". This is worth more than any detail.
- **Do not stop for unfamiliar terminology.** A passage about protein folding will define
  what it needs you to know, and the rest is scenery.

## Answering

- **Go back to the passage for every question.** Every one. Memory of a passage you read 90
  seconds ago is unreliable in a way that does not feel unreliable.
- **Find the specific lines that justify your answer.** If you cannot point at them, you
  are guessing from impression, which is exactly what the wrong options are built for.
- **Answer in your own words first**, then look for the option closest to it — the same
  discipline as [Finding The Clue Word](./Finding%20The%20Clue%20Word.md).

## Pacing on a set

The first question on a long passage is expensive because it includes the reading. The
rest are cheap. Budget about three minutes to read a long passage, then a minute per
question.

If time is short, **do the short passages first** — each question is worth the same, and
the short ones cost far less per point. See [When To Skip](./When%20To%20Skip.md).

## The most common wrong answer

"True but not asked." It is supported by the passage and answers a different question.
When two options both look defensible, re-read the *question stem*, not the passage: one
of them is answering something you were not asked. See
[Trap Answer Patterns](./Trap%20Answer%20Patterns.md).

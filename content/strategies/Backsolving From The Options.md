## The idea

You are rarely asked to *produce* a number. You are asked to *identify* one. When the
options are numbers and the algebra is awkward, working backwards from the options is
often faster and far less error-prone than solving forwards.

## The method

1. GRE options are listed in ascending order. **Start with the middle one.**
2. Test it against the conditions in the problem.
3. Too big? The answer lies below it, and you have eliminated three options with one test.
   Too small? You have eliminated three the other way.
4. Test one of the two survivors.

Two tests, worst case, on a five-option question.

## When it shines

- Word problems where translating into algebra takes longer than checking a candidate.
- "Which of the following is a solution to..." — literally an instruction to substitute.
- Problems with an awkward constraint (must be an integer, must be positive) that is
  trivial to check but painful to impose algebraically.
- Any moment when you can feel the algebra going wrong.

## When to skip it

- The options are expressions rather than numbers. Use
  [Plugging In Numbers](./Plugging%20In%20Numbers.md).
- The options are far apart and estimation settles it faster.
- Testing a single option costs nearly as much work as solving outright.

## Why this is not cheating

There is no credit for method on this test. The score counts correct answers under a
clock. A technique that gets the right answer in 40 seconds instead of 90 is not a
shortcut around the skill being measured — under these conditions it *is* the skill being
measured.
